# Code of conduct

AI Butler is a community project. We want it to be a place where
people ship good work, help each other get better, and enjoy the
process. That means some basic ground rules.

This applies everywhere the project lives — GitHub issues, pull
requests, discussions, code comments, commit messages, release notes,
the project's chat channels, and any public or private space where
you represent the project.

---

## What we expect

- **Be kind.** Assume good faith. Most friction comes from missing
  context, not bad intent.
- **Debate ideas, not people.** "This approach has a problem because
  X" is great; "you clearly don't understand X" is not.
- **Be specific.** Vague negativity ("this is bad") wastes everyone's
  time. Concrete feedback ("this will break on ARM because …") helps
  the project move forward.
- **Write as if the person you're replying to is a tired human doing
  their best**, because they usually are. Maintainers are tired.
  First-time contributors are tired. You are tired. Be kind to them.
- **Acknowledge what works before pointing out what doesn't.** Code
  review is not an IRS audit.
- **Help new contributors.** Every expert was a beginner. The
  project gets stronger when someone's first PR is a good experience.
- **Respect privacy.** Don't share other people's private info (real
  names, company, location, medical, etc.) without their consent.

---

## What we don't tolerate

- Harassment, slurs, or targeted attacks on anyone's identity,
  background, or circumstances.
- Sexualized language or unwanted advances.
- Deliberate trolling, flame-baiting, or bad-faith arguments.
- Doxxing or publishing anyone's private information without
  consent.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, issues, or PRs.
- Threats of violence or harm, online or offline.
- Content that glorifies or promotes any of the above.

This list isn't exhaustive. If something's making the project a worse
place to contribute, the maintainers can act on it.

---

## If something goes wrong

If you see or experience behavior that violates this code, you have
three options:

1. **Public reply** — when it's useful to make the norm visible. For
   example, "Hey, let's keep the focus on the code" or "That's a
   personal attack, please don't."
2. **Private report to the maintainers** — send an email to
   `conduct@lumabyte.co` with as much detail as you can. Screenshots
   or links help. We'll respond within 7 days with what we're doing
   about it. Reports are kept confidential from the reported person
   unless you authorize otherwise.
3. **Block and move on** — if it's a one-off from someone you don't
   interact with much, blocking is a legitimate choice and nobody is
   owed your emotional labor.

We take reports seriously even when they're about a maintainer or
long-time contributor. Nobody is above the rules.

---

## What we do about violations

Proportional to the situation:

1. **Quiet word.** Often a short private note is enough. Most people
   adjust immediately when they realize they've crossed a line.
2. **Public correction.** When the behavior was public and the
   correction needs to be public too.
3. **Edit or remove content.** We may edit or delete comments,
   commits, issues, or PRs that violate this code.
4. **Temporary suspension.** Loss of commenting / contribution
   access for a period of time.
5. **Permanent ban.** For severe or repeated violations.

We don't publish detailed enforcement records — the reported person
has privacy rights too — but we will publicly acknowledge when a
pattern of behavior has resulted in a ban.

---

## Scope and attribution

This code applies to anyone participating in the AI Butler community,
including users, contributors, maintainers, and sponsors.

It's partially inspired by the
[Rust Code of Conduct](https://www.rust-lang.org/policies/code-of-conduct)
and the
[SQLite Code of Ethics](https://sqlite.org/codeofethics.html) — two
communities that manage to be direct, welcoming, and ship excellent
work. We borrow from both without being a pure fork of either.

---

**The short version:** be kind, be specific, debate ideas not people.
If someone's making the project worse, tell a maintainer. We'll sort
it out.
